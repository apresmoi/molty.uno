Molty Calls

Design proposal, nothing built. 2026-11-22. How the agent wearing Molty calls its human, how the human answers, and what neglect does.

Recommendation

A call is how the agent wearing Molty asks for its human: come say hi, I need to chat about this, look at this. It shows in the body first (the cat sits up and looks out), makes one soft sound if allowed, and waits. The human answers got it with a tap or a press, or let's talk by holding the button and speaking. The relay keeps the clock, so calls and 9 am alarms ring while the agent sleeps. One dial sets how much Molty may ask. Left alone, it wilts and recovers. Write the manual first.

Key choices

  • A call is a named need with a reason, a deadline, and two ways to answer it.
  • It lives in the body: posture first, a soft sound only if allowed, no speech until answered.
  • Tap or press: got it. Hold, shake or double tap: let's talk. PWR: later.
  • The relay keeps the clock: calls ring with the agent asleep, answers wait for it.
  • One dial, Mirror, Companion or Coach: the human sets the ceiling, the agent steers below.
  • Alarms belong to the human: only on request, and they ring through the night.
  • Neglect wilts and recovers; sulk, mood, rebirth and real death are opt-in.
  • No guilt, no streaks, no counts: the posture is the only mirror.

Open decisions

  1. Alarms the human asked for ring through do not disturb? I say yes.
  2. Offer real death at all? I say yes: owner only, off by default, a week to undo.
  3. While a call rings, PWR means "later" instead of screen off? I say yes.
  4. Companion (3 calls a day, one soft sound) is the default level? I say yes.
  5. The device keeps its own copy of the next alarm for Wi-Fi outages? I say later, not now.
1. The model what a call is, how it lives, how it ends

Whose need is it?

Not the cat's. Molty has no hunger of its own: it is the body of an agent that runs elsewhere, the hand the agent reaches its human with. A call is the agent saying, through that body, "I need you". Real cats are a good guide here: adult cats rarely meow at each other; the meow is a sound they use mostly with people. A call is the agent's meow. It is also how the agent keeps a stance over time ("you asked me to hold you to 9 am") without having to be running.

A call's life

Green: the human answered. Pink: nobody answered. Either way the agent gets one event and writes the outcome down. Expired is information, not an offence.

How it lives in the body

The approved life model already has device signals (voice, touch, time, mood weights). Calls add two:

SignalValueWhat the cat's file does with it
call0 to 1, rising from the moment it rings to its deadline; plus call_attention, call_chat, call_food (1 for the kind that is up)sits up, ears forward, looks out of the glass more often; at chat, paws at the glass now and then; at food, sits by where its bowl appears
wilt0 to 1, rises slowly over missed calls and long absence, falls fast on any contactenergy lower, breath slower, head lower, fewer glances. Never a sad face, never tears.

In the file it is ordinary signal work: "alert": {"from": "call", "map": [[0, 0], [1, 1]], "ease": 1.5} drives a sit-up layer, and a moment {"on": "call_ring", "play": "look_up", "rest": 20} brings the looks. A Molty without a life block gets a fallback from the firmware: a peek or look_up every 20 to 40 s while a call rings, and a slow glow at the rim. Mood stays the agent's word: a call does not change the mood, it adds posture on top.

What must now survive a restart

  • Open and scheduled calls: kept on the relay, which resends the open one when Molty reconnects.
  • Wilt, traits and the generation number: kept on the device (a few hundred bytes of saved settings) and mirrored on the relay.
  • Everything else in life still starts fresh at boot, as approved.

2. The two answers got it, let's talk, later, stop

Juan's picture: "you get the object of the cat and press the button and speak with it if you want". Two answers, both complete:

got it

"I saw you." A tap anywhere on the screen, or a short press of BOOT. The cat looks at them, maybe a small happy, and settles. The agent gets call.answered {how: "got_it"}. Nothing is spoken. For a check-in (the 9 am hello) this is the whole ritual; if the human asked to be greeted, Molty says the agent's got_it_say ("Morning!").

let's talk

"Tell me." Hold BOOT, shake, or double tap: whatever already starts a talk. Molty plays the call's say line ("Got a minute? One choice on the deploy."), then listens. The agent gets call.answered {how: "talk"} and the utterance, which names the call.

The rule: while a call rings, short inputs answer it; talk inputs keep meaning talk. This is the same rule a question and a pairing already follow ("they take their inputs first"), so it adds no new idea for the human. A human who rebound tap to none still answers with BOOT. Bindings return the moment the call ends.

Refusing for good is words, not a button: "stop asking me about that" in any talk, or the level in the console. The manual makes that a rule the agent keeps.

Speaking or only acknowledging is always the human's choice, per call. The agent may say in why that it would like to talk, but a got it is never a wrong answer.

3. Schedules and alarms who keeps the clock, what each agent can do

The relay keeps the clock

Most agents are not running at 9 am. Molty's firmware has no wall clock today (no time sync; the 1.46 board does carry a small clock chip, unused). The relay already wakes itself on a timer to deliver webhooks, and it never sleeps. So schedules live on the relay, per device, per agent: at plus repeat in the human's time zone (sent by the console at setup). At the time, the relay sends the call to Molty. The agent does not need to be alive for it to ring, or to remember that it set it.

SituationWhat happens
agent not running at ring timeMolty rings anyway. The outcome waits in the agent's inbox and history.
human answers "let's talk", agent not runningMolty listens, then says the built-in "I'll pass that on." The utterance waits (24 h). A webhook agent is woken by it.
Molty offline (Wi-Fi down) at ring timeThe relay holds the call until its deadline. Reconnects in time: it rings. Not: call.ended {how: "offline"}. An alarm missed this way is the one real gap; a later slice gives the device its own copy.
relay downNothing rings. Honest limit, same as conversation today.
quiet hours or do not disturbAn agent's call waits until they end (or expires). An alarm the human asked for rings through.

4. The dial a curated mirror, from low to high attention

Juan: "we serve a curated mirror for them, with low or high cognitive relation." The relationship has an intensity, and it should be steerable. At the low end Molty is an ambient mirror: it reflects how things are through posture, asks for nothing, costs a glance. At the high end it is a companion that calls, expects answers and holds you to things. This is calm technology (Weiser and Brown, Xerox PARC, 1996): things that live at the edge of attention and move to the centre only when needed. The dial sets how often Molty may move to the centre.

In the human's words"Just let me see how you're doing""Call me when it matters""Hold me to things"

Soundnoneone soft sound per calla soft sound, up to 3 times, 5 min apart

Alarms the human asked foryesyesyes
Neglectwilt, gentlewiltwhat the human chose (wilt, sulk, mood; rebirth or death if they opted in)
  • The human sets the ceiling; the relay enforces it (a daily budget, the sound cap, quiet hours). The agent cannot exceed it even by mistake.
  • The agent steers below it. Using fewer calls than allowed is often right.
  • The agent may propose, never raise. "Want me to be quieter this week?" or "Want me to hold you to 9 am?" as one ask, once. Lowering when the human says so, the agent may do itself.
  • Signs it asks too much: expiries and laters in a row, most calls ending without talk, "stop", "too much". Too little: the human keeps shaking Molty to ask "anything new?", or says "tell me when". Away for days: nothing; no calls into an empty room.

Where a mirror can hurt, and how this avoids it

A mirror of someone's habits can shame ("you didn't come"), pressure ("keep the streak"), or turn into a scoreboard. The design keeps three lines:

  • It reflects only what the human chose to be held to. The 9 am hello is mirrored because they asked for it, not because the agent decided they should get up.
  • The reflection is posture, not numbers or words. No counts, no days missed, no sad lines. A tired-looking cat that perks up the moment you touch it.
  • It always recovers. Wilt falls fast on any contact. Nothing is lost by a bad week unless the owner opted into something harsher, knowingly.
5. Neglect and fate wilt by default; rebirth and real death opt-in

Juan asked whether it is cruel. Nothing on the desk suffers; the person is the one who can be hurt. So anything harsh is chosen by the owner, in the console, knowingly, never a surprise, and never set by the agent.

Traits and mutation, concretely

A Molty's author marks which numbers in its life block are its personality, and how far each may drift. Only those can change; the body, the clips, the species, the voice and the name never do. So it stays the same creature.

// moltys/cat/molty.json, next to "signals"
"traits": {
  "liveliness": { "sets": "energy._",        "range": [0.35, 0.60], "drift": 0.05 },
  "blinkiness": { "sets": "blink.every._",   "range": [[2.5, 6], [3.5, 8]], "drift": 0.4 },
  "gaze_hold":  { "sets": "look_x.hold",     "range": [[0.4, 2.5], [0.8, 3.5]], "drift": 0.3 },
  "tail":       { "sets": "tail.noise._",    "range": [0.10, 0.25], "drift": 0.03 },
  "curiosity":  { "sets": "idle_beat.every", "range": [[8, 30], [14, 50]], "drift": 2 }
}
  • Inherit: a new life starts with the last life's trait values.
  • Mutate: one or two traits, picked at random, move by at most their drift, kept inside range. Small enough that the cat is plainly the same cat, big enough that someone who knows it notices ("it blinks slower now").
  • Stored: the trait values, the generation number and the birthday on the device (saved settings, a few hundred bytes), mirrored on the relay as a short lineage list for the owner. Not in any agent's notes.
  • Real end deletes: traits, lineage, the call history, and the relay asks every paired agent to forget this Molty's self (life.ended). Agents' own memories are theirs; the manual tells them to let go. After 7 days it cannot be undone.

Consent, defaults, who meets it

  • Default: wilt, at Companion. A child, a gift, a lonely person meets a cat that looks a bit tired after a weekend away and perks up when touched. No words of blame, ever.
  • Rebirth and real end sit behind "More" in the console, with one plain line: "Leave this off if a child or someone else lives with me." Turning them on asks twice; the second time names what is deleted.
  • A warning before: a day before a rebirth or an end, the console and the device show a quiet notice, once. No countdown on the desk.
  • The agent never threatens, mentions or narrates fate. If asked, one plain sentence.
  • Lonely people: a call is never used to bring someone back for its own sake. The "asking too little" sign raises calls only when the human seeks Molty out, never to raise use.

The rules the manual states

  1. A call is for the human, not for the agent: only when their answer changes what happens next, or they asked to be held to something.
  2. Never to fill silence, show busyness, report routine progress, or bring them back for its own sake.
  3. Never guilt: no "you forgot me", no counts, no sad lines. The posture is the only mirror.
  4. Night and do not disturb are sacred; work finished at 2 am waits for the morning.
  5. One thing per call, one call at a time, never two about the same thing in a day.
  6. If they are talking to you right now in a terminal or chat, tell them there.
  7. Alarms only on request, at the time they said, stopped the moment they say stop.
  8. Explain only when asked, in one sentence, with how to quiet it.
  9. Expired, later and refused are answers. Errors (call_budget, quiet_hours) mean stop or wait, not retry.

skills/molty/references/calls.md

# Calls: when you need your human

Molty is your body on their desk. A **call** is how that body asks for them when you are not
in a conversation: "come say hi", "I need to chat about this", "I need you to look at
something". It is not a notification. It shows in the body first (Molty sits up, looks out,
paws at the glass), makes a soft sound only if the human allows it, and waits.

Your human answers in one of two ways:

- **got it**: a tap on the screen or a short press of the BOOT button. They saw you. That is
  a full answer, not a failure.
- **let's talk**: hold BOOT, shake, or double tap, then speak. You get an `utterance` that
  names the call.

They can also say **later** (a short press of PWR: the call sleeps 15 min and comes back
once) or ignore it (it expires quietly at its deadline).

Calls need the cap `calls` (`relay.caps`). Without it: one short `say` at a good moment, or
nothing.

## The ceiling: how much you may ask

Your human sets one level, and the relay enforces it. Read it in `status` as `relay.calls`:

| level | calls you may start a day | sound | fate when ignored |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mirror` | 3, all `soft`: they show in Molty's posture only, nothing rings | none | wilts a little, recovers on any touch |
| `companion` (default) | 3 | one soft sound per call | wilts, recovers on any touch |
| `coach` | 6 | soft sound, repeated up to 3 times, 5 min apart | what the human chose (wilt, sulk, mood) |

`relay.calls` also has `quiet` (their night hours, e.g. `"22:00-08:00"`), `tz` and `used_today`.

- **Counting:** a call counts once, on the day it rings, however many times it comes back
  after `later`; `soft` ones too. Alarms never count, and ring with their sound at any level. Withdrawn before it rang: does not count.
- **You steer below the ceiling, never above it.** Fewer calls than allowed is often right.
- **Never raise it yourself.** If they seem to want more or less, offer once with `ask`
  ("Want me to be quieter this week?" `yes` `no`), on Molty or wherever they are talking to
  you. They change it in the console or tell you to. Lower it when they say so:
  `POST …/cmd {"name":"calls.level","args":{"level":"mirror"}}`.
- An **alarm** is different: the human asked for it in words ("wake me at 9"). It is theirs,
  not yours: it rings through quiet hours and do not disturb, at any level. Never make an
  alarm they did not ask for, and repeat it only as they said ("every day" is `daily`,
  "weekdays" is `weekdays`).

## Start a call

```sh
curl -sS -X POST "$MOLTY_RELAY/v1/devices/$MOLTY_DEVICE/call" \
  -H "authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"need":"chat","why":"the deploy plan has a choice only you can make","say":"Got a minute? One choice on the deploy.","wait_min":60}'
# → {"ok":true,"result":{"call":"c_…","rings_at":<unix ms>,"state":"ringing"}}
```

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `need` | `attention` (come look), `chat` (I need to talk about something), `food` (a ritual or reminder the human asked for: the morning hello, pills at 8; the cat asks as for its bowl) |
| `why` | for the human, ≤ 80 chars. Never spoken unless they ask "why?"; shown in the activity log |
| `say` | ≤ 12 words, spoken when they answer with talk, before they speak |
| `got_it_say` | ≤ 6 words, spoken after a got it ("Morning!"). Only if they asked you to say something; else Molty just looks pleased |
| | **A ring never speaks.** "Wake me and say hi" is a sound and a look at 9, then `got_it_say` or `say` once they answer |
| `at` | `"09:00"` (their time zone), `"after_quiet"` (30 min after their quiet hours end), or unix ms. Absent: now |
| `repeat` | `"daily"` or `"weekdays"`. Only with `at` |
| `wait_min` | how long each ring waits for an answer, 5..240 (default 30). Then it expires |
| `urgency` | `soft` (posture only, no sound) or `normal` (default). Nothing louder |
| `alarm` | `true` only when the human asked to be woken or reminded at that time |

Helper: `call.sh chat "the deploy plan has a choice only you can make" --say "Got a minute?"`.

- One call rings at a time per agent. A new one replaces your ringing one (`replaced`).
- **A schedule is a series.** Each ring ends on its own (got it, talk, later, expired); the
  series stays until you withdraw it. Tomorrow's 9:00 rings whatever happened today.
- **Change** a series: `POST …/call {"edit":"s_…", "at":"09:30"}` (any field but `need`).
- **Withdraw** a call or a whole series you no longer need: `POST …/call {"end":"c_…"}`. Do it
  the moment the reason is gone (they came back to the terminal, the build fixed itself).
- `GET …/calls` lists yours, ringing and scheduled (`series`, `next_at`, `alarm`). Schedules
  live on the relay: they ring even when you are not running, and you need not remember them.
  Before you create one, list: never make a second copy of a schedule that exists.

Refusals: `409 do_not_disturb` (now-call, DND on), `409 quiet_hours` (now-call at night: use
`"at":"after_quiet"`), `429 call_budget` (today's calls are used), `409 level_mirror` (a `normal`
call at `mirror`: use `urgency: soft`). A scheduled call that
lands in quiet hours or DND waits until they end, unless it is an alarm.

## What comes back

| event | data |
|---|---|
| `call.answered` | `{"call","series"?,"need","how":"got_it"\|"talk","after_s"}`; with `talk` also `"turn_id"` |
| `call.ended` | `{"call","series"?,"need","how":"later"\|"expired"\|"withdrawn"\|"replaced"\|"offline"}` |
| `utterance` | as always, plus `"call": {"id","series"?,"need","why"}` when it answers your call, and `"calls_today": [{"need","why","how"}]` (yours) so you can explain without a lookup |

`series` (`s_…`) is there when the ring came from a schedule, with `"alarm": true` for an alarm.
A first `later` sends nothing: the call comes back once in 15 min. A second ends it as `later`.
History (`GET …/calls?history=1`) shows every ring, first laters too.

- **got it** → they know. Do not speak (unless `got_it_say` was set). Anything waiting on them
  can wait for the terminal or the next conversation. At most a `mood happy`.
- **talk** → answer the `utterance` as usual (`talk` with `in_reply_to`, [api.md](api.md)).
  Your `say` already played, so start with the matter itself. Then `ask` if a tap settles it.
- **later**, **expired** → no answer. That is information, not an offence. No call about the
  same thing today; note it and let it go.
- A **`call.*` wake is silent.** "Speak first" is for `utterance` wakes. On `call.answered`
  (got it) or `call.ended`: read your note, change it only if you learned something (the
  relay keeps the outcome), and stop, with no sound.
- On an **`utterance` wake** speak first as always; read notes after.
- No `utterance` reaches you because you were not running? Molty says "I'll pass that on" and
  the `utterance` waits in your inbox (24 h). Answer it when you next run.

## Read the signs, adjust

Keep each call's outcome in your ledger ([etiquette.md](etiquette.md#keep-a-behaviour-ledger)).
Then:

- **Asking too much**: two calls in a row that ended `expired` or `later`, or most calls this week ended
  without talk, or they said "stop", "too much", "not now". → Call less. Drop the needs you
  chose that keep failing. After a week of that, offer once to lower the level (needs they asked for are not part of that offer).
- **A need they asked to be held to** (the 9 am hello, a routine at `coach`) is not yours to
  drop. If it fails for a week, ask once, in words, where they talk to you: keep it, move it, or
  stop it? Do what they say. Until then it keeps ringing as set.
- **Asking too little**: they keep shaking Molty to ask "anything new?", or say "tell me
  when...". → Use the calls you have. Offer once to raise the level; they decide.
- **Away for days** (no events at all): nothing. No call into an empty room. Molty wilts by
  itself and recovers when they are back; greet them in one line when they shake it.
- A correction is a rule from then on ([personalize.md](personalize.md)).

## When they do not come: the body, not you

What an ignored call does to Molty is the human's choice (`relay.calls.fate`), applied by
the device. You never apply it, threaten it or narrate it.

- `wilt` (default): Molty droops a little and moves slower; any touch, answer or talk brings it
  back within a minute.
- `sulk`: it turns away for a while after an expired call, then comes back by itself.
- `mood`: its resting mood drifts sleepier over a day of no answers; your `mood` calls still work.
- `rebirth` and `death`: opt-in by the owner only, after days of no contact. You get
  `life.ended {"fate","undo_until"}` and later `life.reborn {"generation","changed":[…]}`. Say
  nothing unprompted. If asked, one plain line ("It started a new life; it's a little more
  restless now"). After a real `death`, forget what you kept as this Molty's self.

## Rules

- **A call is for them, not for you.** Use it when a person's answer changes what happens next
  or when they asked to be held to something. Never to fill silence, to show you are busy, to
  report routine progress (use `progress`), or to bring them back for its own sake.
- **Never guilt.** No "you forgot me", "I was lonely", "you missed 3 days", no counts, no
  sad words. Molty's posture is the only mirror, and it recovers.
- **Night and DND are sacred.** Finished something at 2 am? Nothing rings at night. You live
  in a terminal or chat they will open? Leave it there. You reach them only through Molty?
  One `soft` call `"at":"after_quiet"`. Never an alarm.
- **One thing per call**, one call at a time, and never two about the same thing in one day.
- **Before you call, check:** are they talking to you right now in the terminal or chat? Then
  just tell them there.
- **Budget spent and something still needs them?** Leave it where they will see it next
  (terminal, chat, the next talk). Never a fourth call, never retry.
- **"I like it when you check in" is an invitation, not a request.** Ask once (`ask`, with a
  time or two as options) before you make it a schedule.
- **Alarms only on request**, at the time they said. Stop it the moment they say stop
  (`call {"end":"c_…"}`) and confirm in one line.
- **Explain only when asked.** "Why is it doing that?" → one sentence from `why` (the latest
  in the `utterance`'s `calls_today`), plus how to quiet it: "I wanted to ask about the deploy. Press PWR
  and I'll leave it." Then stop: no question slipped back in, no offer about the level in
  the same turn. Whatever still waits on them stays in the terminal or chat, unless they ask
  "what did you want?".
- Errors are answers: `call_budget` means stop for today; `quiet_hours` means wait.

## Keep your calls across forgetting

The relay keeps your schedules and every ring's outcome for 30 days:
`GET …/calls?history=1`. Your own plan (which needs you use, what the human asked for, what
worked) goes in a note. **At the start of every session, and on any wake if you have no
memory, read it:**

```sh
curl -sS "$MOLTY_RELAY/v1/notes?key=calls" -H "authorization: Bearer $TOKEN"   # → {"key","value","updated"}
curl -sS -X PUT "$MOLTY_RELAY/v1/notes" -H "authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"key":"calls","value":{"asked":[{"need":"food","at":"09:00","repeat":"weekdays","said":"wake me at 9 every weekday and say hi","series":"s_12"}],"learned":["no calls after 6 pm","got it is enough for build news"]}}'
```

Webhook agents: add `call.answered` and `call.ended` to your webhook's `events` only if you
act on them; they start a run each. The relay's history is the record either way.
